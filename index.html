welcome to my website's about section :p here i will very transparently talk about the making of this site, (as a way of documenting it), the inspirations behind it, creative process, the resources i used, and other things.

why cloverlover...?
if i'm being honest... i like clovers and needed something catchy that rhymed. other possible names were 'cleverclover' which is actually really cool now that i think about it, reason why it stayed as my atabook name.

the rough 'sketch'
the idea for my website's layout first started as an ugly hand-drawn sketch in my actual irl note pad, if you're only browsing neocities for the meantime but you're interested in having a website of your own, this part is crutial. before anything, an outline is needed, i would recommend doing this first before hopping on the actual coding part, (even basic tutorials, yes), and after you're satisfied, then you could see if what you're looking for is possible and how is it possible.

inspiration
back in 2022 i came across a really cool tumblr blog with the craziest most cutest layout you could think of, it was chocolatety, pinky, just so so cute. unfortunately i could no longer find it, which is sad and still haunts me (T___T), that i'd say is one of my biggest inspirations to this 'cutesey' style.

other HUGE inspirations are the following tumblr (+ sozai website) blogs, i went with a very very similar theme as you can see :3

this is a compilation of different resources used in the making of this website!

photopea.com: i make pixels and graphics here, very similar to photoshop actually.
w3schools.com: basic html tutorials/help and where i coded my site from (it's a funny story heh).
pinterest.com: got my hexcode 'color pallettes' from here, as well as some images.
foollovers.com: this website's style was also inspired by some themes here! (esp the little lace thingy).

my coding process...?
my website is coded by me from scratch, even if i'm taking inspiration from someone else's work i don't just copy and paste their css/html, and you shouldn't either... not cool (unless it's for educational purposes...? idk), if you had to ask me, i don't really care if people copy my work, but i really encourage you to create something that is euniquely yours :3

1. i always start with divs, my whole website is basically divs, that's what i like to use personally, so you could say it's just 'containers' one after another. i assign classes to all of them or what i like to call: their names, as well as some html notes to tell appart the closing div tags and what div they belong to.
2. css. yes, i start with the css right away lol, i need to know how the wrappers and containers will look like with their designated dimensions. i usually set things to have a grey or pink background-color, just so i can see them better hehe, this is the most creative part of all.
3. the actual content. this is where i write and yap. step 3.5 would be styling all of that hehe, so i make sure everything has a class assigned.
4. i determine what will be javascript (and what will not). after everything is almost coming together i think about how i want to make things interactive, i personally prefer to make 'sections' with js, i also use it for music, sometimes videos and so. i assign 'id's to the elements that require it, and finally i steal... cough i mean i browse and borrow some code from back-end devs that kindly post it for educational purposes.

foodie hall of fame

brownies
★★★★☆
25min - total 50min

ingredients
  • 150g chocolate
  • 90g unsalted butter
  • 1tbs coffee
  • 2 eggs
  • 120g brown sugar
  • 30g flour
  • 20g cocoa powder
  • 2g vanilla
 instructions
  • preheat the oven to 175°C (350°F)
  • in a double boiler melt butter + chocolate
  • in another double boiler dissolve the sugar combined with 2 eggs
  • combine the mixtures together with the vanilla
  • sift and combine dry ingredients
  • mix dry + wet ingredients in a folding motion.
  • set oven to 180°C and bake for 25min

onigiri
★★★★☆
total 30min

ingredients
  • 240g cooked white rice
  • 5g rice vinegar
  • 2g salt & other spices
  • roasted seaweed
  • crab sticks
  • kewpie mayo (or other)
  • chopped veggies
 instructions
  • combine chopped protein with veggies (carrots, cucumbers, etc.)
  • add mayo or preferred product to those
  • add spices (salt, pepper, onion powder, garlic powder or sesame seeds, etc.) and mix well
  • add a tbs or 5g of rice vinegar to the rice
  • for molds: add one layer of rice a layer of filling, and another layer of rice, then press.
  • regular method: flatten a small portion of rice on the palm, add a spoonful of filling, a second layer of rice and shape it using both hands.
  • cut the seaweed to size and put it on the onigiris
